Two fusions, not one

Every raw similarity matrix is fused twice, along two different axes, before S_joint ever exists — this is what makes the two disagreement cues possible below.

per camera  →  S_cam0, S_cam5

Fuses SeqSLAM + DINOv2 within one camera. Comparing the two resulting paths (path_cam0 vs. path_cam5) is cam_disagree — do the two physical viewpoints agree on where we are?

per descriptor  →  S_seqslam, S_dinov2

Fuses cam0 + cam5 within one descriptor. Comparing those two paths (path_seqslam vs. path_dinov2) is desc_disagree — do the classical and the learned descriptor agree?

Four independent checks, one abstention rule

Computed only after path_joint is fixed — none of the four can see or influence the DTW search itself, so a row failing all four is a genuinely different signal than a row that just has low raw similarity.

ridge_z

Mean of S_joint along the path's own diagonal trajectory, ±5 rows. Is the claimed match strong on its own terms?

margin_z

Claimed similarity minus the best alternative outside a ±30-column band. Strong but not distinctive reads differently from strong and distinctive.

cam_disagree

|path_cam0 − path_cam5| in columns, this row. Two independent physical views of the same place.

desc_disagree

|path_seqslam − path_dinov2| in columns, this row. A classical and a learned read of the same pixels.
